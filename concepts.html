Direction B — the pick

Paint one wedge.
Watch the symmetry finish it for you.

Try it — tap a wedge in the rosette. The same stroke mirrors across all six automatically, driven by the same rotation/reflection matrices already in wallpaperGroups.js. That single mechanic — one honest tap producing a whole symmetric bloom — is the game.

No brush skill required, no way to make it "look wrong." Every session ends at something finished and worth a screenshot.

Star & Strap

The rosette as a daily puzzle

A new girih star rosette every day — pentagon core, ring of bowties and kites radiating out. Fill each cell; the strapwork lines only look "finished" once every tile touching that line agrees on tone.

  • Daily rosette, larger growth rings unlock as streak builds
  • Scored on palette harmony, not speed
  • Finished rosettes save as a growing gallery wall
Engine reuse
girihTiles.js — tile geometry + strapwork
Direction B Built · cherrypow.github.io/ZenColour

Wallpaper Bloom

One tap, six-fold bloom — shown live above

Player only ever touches a single unit cell; the chosen wallpaper group does the rest. Swap symmetry groups (p6m, p4m, cmm...) to change the whole feel of the piece without re-coloring anything.

  • Lowest skill floor of the three — hardest to "mess up"
  • 17 groups already coded = 17 free level themes
  • Native fit for a satisfying screen-recording share moment
Engine reuse
wallpaperGroups.js — 17-group replicator

Quasicrystal Drift

A pattern that never once repeats

Penrose's rhombus tiling — already coded as penroseTiles() — has the one property none of the others do: however far you tile it, no finite patch ever recurs. Coloring becomes a "no two regions are really alike" meditation instead of a symmetry payoff.

  • Zero new math — patterns.js already generates it via Robinson-triangle deflation
  • Slower, more contemplative than A or B — thin/thick rhombi only, no strapwork
  • Natural "depth" dial: each deflation level = a harder, denser board
Engine reuse
patterns.js — penroseTiles(), deflate()
Direction E Concept only

Recursive Bloom

The rosette, inside the rosette, inside the rosette

Not a new fractal library — the existing wedge motif calling itself. Color the outer ring, and a shrunken copy of the same rosette appears inside each cell you just filled, at 40% scale, one level deeper. Three or four levels down before it's too small to tap.

  • Built entirely from primitives A/B already use — no new renderer
  • Self-similarity gives it a real "zoom forever" feeling other modes don't have
  • Natural pacing: each level is a smaller, faster, calmer decision
Engine reuse
rosetteCellTiles() called recursively per-cell

On "fractal," specifically

Worth being precise before building E, because "fractal" points at two genuinely different things — engine-wise and feel-wise:

→ what E, above, is Self-similar tessellation. Discrete polygons that contain smaller copies of themselves, N levels deep — a rep-tile, same family as Penrose and the girih rosette. Renders with the exact same canvas-polygon pipeline as A/B/D. Tap targets stay crisp shapes at every depth.
→ the other meaning Escape-time fractal. Mandelbrot/Julia/flame-style — per-pixel iteration, not polygons. Genuinely more "infinite zoom," but it's a different renderer from scratch (no reuse from tessellate/), and coloring means assigning palette to iteration-count bands rather than tapping a region, which is a different game feel entirely.

Recommendation: build E as the self-similar version first — it's nearly free given A/B already exist, and it's a genuine mechanic, not just a visual. The escape-time route is worth a sixth concept later if the zoom itself is what you want to chase.
