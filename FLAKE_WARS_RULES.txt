FLAKE WARS — RULES (v10)
=========================

THE BOARD
---------
- 13 "flowers," each a cluster of 7 hexagons (1 center + 6 petals) = 91
  cells total. The flowers themselves are arranged in a hex pattern: 1
  center flower, 6 flowers around it, and 6 more nestled in the gaps
  between those (each of those 6 touches its two flower-neighbors, not
  just one).
- Every cell gets a number from 1-7 before play starts, generated once
  and never changed. Each flower independently contains every number
  1-7 exactly once (like a mini Sudoku box).
- The whole board is visible and numbered from the very first move —
  nothing is hidden. Unclaimed cells show a faint tint identifying
  which flower they belong to.

TURNS
-----
- You and the AI alternate. On your turn, tap any unclaimed cell to
  claim it — its number becomes your points (see SCORING).
- There is no "which number to place" — the number was already fixed
  at generation. The only decision is which CELL to claim.
- Undo steps back one move if you want to review what happened.

SHADE
-----
- Each player's own claims alternate between two shades automatically,
  based on THAT PLAYER'S own move count (not the global turn number):
  your 1st claim is dark, 2nd is light, 3rd dark again, and so on.
  Same for the AI, independently.
- You do not choose your shade — it's forced by how many moves you've
  made. This matters for bridges (below).
- Cells claimed via Envelopment (see below) never get a shade at all,
  since they weren't part of a deliberate alternating tap.

BRIDGES (+10 points)
---------------------
A bridge forms between two of YOUR OWN cells when ALL of these hold:
  1. They're adjacent across a FLOWER BOUNDARY (never two cells
     within the same flower — every flower already contains all of
     1-7, so same-flower neighbors are guaranteed to include numbers
     that would "match" by coincidence; that's noise, not strategy).
  2. Their numbers are the SAME, or they're a complementary pair:
     1↔7, 2↔6, 3↔5. (4 has no partner — it can only bridge to
     another 4.)
  3. Their SHADES ALTERNATE — one dark, one light. Two dark cells (or
     two light cells) do NOT bridge, even with matching numbers.
- A completed bridge is drawn as a gold line directly connecting the
  two cells, so it's always visible why points were scored.
- Because shade is forced by move-count and not chosen, a bridge you're
  building is telegraphed — the opponent can see it coming and has a
  window to interfere before your shade cycles back around.

BOX COMPLETION (+15 points)
-----------------------------
- Own all 7 cells in a single flower (by any mix of direct claims and
  envelopment) and you get +15 for that flower.
- If a flower ends up with mixed ownership when its last cell is
  filled, nobody gets the bonus — it's genuinely contested, not
  awarded to whoever happened to place last.

ENVELOPMENT (free cells)
--------------------------
- After every move, any UNCLAIMED cell touching 3 or more of your
  cells — a strict majority, more than the other player has touching
  it — automatically becomes yours. Free, permanent, the other player
  can never take it.
- This can cascade: claiming one cell can push a neighboring cell over
  the 3-neighbor threshold too, which can push another, and so on. It
  resolves fully before the turn passes.
- Enveloped cells never get a shade, so they can't participate in
  bridges — only in value and box-completion scoring.
- Hint rings show the SINGLE biggest envelopment opportunity currently
  on the board for each side (not every cell that qualifies, which
  buried the signal in noise on a 91-cell board):
    - GOLD ring = the AI's best opportunity right now.
    - PURPLE ring = yours.

CHECK (-8 point penalty)
--------------------------
- If an empty cell already touches 2 of the OPPONENT'S cells (one
  short of an envelopment), that cell is "at risk" — the opponent is
  one claim away from taking it, one way or another.
- If it's your turn and there's a cell at risk from the opponent, and
  your move doesn't leave that specific cell claimed — either by you
  directly, or swept up as a side effect of your own envelopment
  cascade — you take an 8-point penalty.
- Each distinct at-risk cell only ever costs this penalty ONCE. If you
  ignore the same lingering threat on a later turn too, it doesn't
  charge you again — the first miss is the one that counts.
- This is symmetric: the AI evaluates every move through the same
  scoring function you're scored with, so it can walk into this
  penalty too if something else looks more valuable in the moment.

SCORING SUMMARY
-----------------
Your total score =
    (sum of the numbers on cells you own)
  + 10 x (number of bridges you've formed)
  + 15 x (flowers you've fully and solely completed)
  - 8 x (distinct checks you've failed to answer)

Highest total when the board fills wins. Ties are a draw.

THE AI
------
- Evaluates every legal cell each turn by simulating the actual
  outcome (including envelopment cascades and check penalties) and
  comparing it to the current score — the exact same formula above,
  not a separate heuristic.
- It also weighs denying you a good cell (half credit for whatever it
  would have been worth to you), and specifically avoids leaving a
  flower at 6-of-7 filled unless it's the move that completes it
  (handing you a free finish next turn is the single biggest unforced
  error available).
- It only plays its calculated best move some of the time (currently
  15%) — the rest of the time it plays a random legal cell. This is
  deliberate: even a small amount of calculated play overwhelms an
  opponent who isn't specifically hunting for bridges/envelopment/
  checks, so full-strength play produced a near-unbeatable AI in
  testing. This setting can be tuned up or down.

WHAT'S NOT IN HERE (tried and reverted)
------------------------------------------
- A "network/chain" bonus for connecting 3+ bridges in a row, and
  later a "fork" bonus for one cell touching 2+ bridges at once — both
  are mathematically impossible on this board. The cross-flower
  neighbor graph is a proven perfect matching: every cell has at most
  ONE neighbor in a different flower. No cell can ever be part of two
  bridges simultaneously, so any bonus shaped like that is permanent
  dead code. A real fork/network mechanic would need either a
  different board topology, or a definition that combines DIFFERENT
  systems on one cell (e.g. a move that's both a bridge AND an
  envelopment trigger) rather than stacking two of the same thing.
